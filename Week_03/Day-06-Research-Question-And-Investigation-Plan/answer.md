# Day 06 — Research Question and Investigation Plan

## Research Topic

Phishing Attacks

## Background

Phishing is a type of cyberattack where an attacker tries to trick a person into sharing sensitive information, such as passwords, bank details, or OTPs.
Attackers commonly use fake emails, messages, websites, or social media messages that look like they are coming from a trusted person or company. The main goal is to make the victim trust the message and take an unsafe action.

## Research Question

**What are the common signs that can help a user identify a phishing message?**

## Why This Question?

I selected this question because phishing is one of the common cybersecurity threats faced by normal users.
I want to understand how a phishing message looks different from a genuine message and what signs a person should check before clicking a link or sharing any information.

## Current Knowledge

I already know that phishing messages can be sent through email, SMS, social media, or other communication platforms.
I also know that attackers may use fake links, urgent messages, or false offers to make people take action without thinking carefully.

## Assumptions

I think that most phishing messages contain some warning signs, such as suspicious links, spelling mistakes, unusual email addresses, or urgent requests.
I also assume that checking the sender and the link before clicking can help users avoid many phishing attempts. I need to verify these assumptions using reliable sources.

## Sources I Plan to Use

1. **CISA (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency)** — for phishing awareness and security guidance.
2. **Microsoft Security resources** — for information about phishing and common attack techniques.
3. **Google Safety and Security resources** — for information about identifying suspicious messages and links.

## Investigation Plan

First, I will learn the basic concept of phishing and understand why attackers use it. Next, I will research different types of phishing messages, such as email phishing, SMS phishing, and fake login pages. Then, I will identify the common warning signs found in phishing messages and compare them with genuine messages.
I will use information from multiple reliable sources to verify my findings. I will only study publicly available examples and will not send phishing messages or collect anyone's personal information.

## Expected Evidence

The evidence that could help answer my research question includes:

* Examples of documented phishing messages.
* Common characteristics of phishing emails and websites.
* Security guidance from trusted organizations.
* Examples of suspicious links or sender addresses.
* Recommendations for safely handling suspicious messages.

## Limitations

I will focus only on identifying common signs of phishing. I will not create or send real phishing messages, collect passwords or personal information, or test phishing attacks against real users or organizations. The investigation will be based on publicly available information and documented examples.

## What I Learned

I learned that a good research question should be specific and easy to investigate. Instead of researching phishing in general, I focused on the signs that can help users identify phishing messages.
I also learned that I should separate what I already know from what I assume and verify important information using reliable sources. Most importantly, security research should be performed safely and within authorized boundaries.
